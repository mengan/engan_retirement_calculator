Retirement Planning Calculator

This is a free, browser-based tool for modeling your retirement finances. Run scenarios, compare strategies, and explore how your savings, expenses, Social Security, and investments could play out over time. All data stays in your browser — nothing is sent to a server.

⚠ Disclaimer This tool is provided for general educational and planning purposes only. It is not financial, tax, or legal advice. Results are estimates based on the assumptions you enter and are not guarantees of future performance. Before making any investment decisions, changes to your retirement contributions, or other major financial choices, please consult a licensed financial adviser, CPA, or attorney.
🔒 Your data stays on your device All information you enter is saved only in your browser's local storage. No data is ever sent to a server, shared with anyone, or stored anywhere outside your own browser. Clearing your browser data will erase your saved inputs.

How to get started

  1. 1
    General Settings Enter your plan horizon, spouse details, salaries, retirement years, and default investment return and inflation ranges.
  2. 2
    Expenses Set your monthly baseline spending, add recurring expenses (healthcare, car payments), and any large one-time costs (new car, home renovation).
  3. 3
    Investment Accounts Add all your accounts — brokerage, 401(k), IRA, Roth, HSA — with current balances and ongoing contributions.
  4. 4
    Tune other tabs Refine Social Security estimates, add real estate, adjust withdrawal strategy and Roth conversion plan, set tax brackets, and run Monte Carlo simulations.
  5. 5
    Review the Summary See your full projection — asset trajectory, income sources, tax burden, and year-by-year table. Use the zoom controls on each chart to explore different time ranges and scales.

Other Defaults

Social Security

Quick estimator using the SSA "bend-point" formula (2024 base). Enter each spouse's average annual career earnings (in today's dollars) and the age they plan to claim. Filing earlier reduces the benefit; filing later (up to age 70) increases it via delayed retirement credits. The estimated benefit feeds into the main projection automatically.

How this works
  • Earnings are capped at the 2024 SS wage base ($168,600).
  • Average Indexed Monthly Earnings (AIME) = average annual earnings ÷ 12.
  • PIA = 90% of first $1,174 of AIME + 32% from $1,174 to $7,078 + 15% above $7,078.
  • Years worked < 35 scales the benefit down proportionally.
  • Claiming at 62 ≈ 70% of FRA benefit; at 67 (FRA) = 100%; at 70 ≈ 124%.
  • Manual override lets you paste in your real SSA statement number.

Investment Accounts

All accounts grow at the Default Investment Return from General Settings. Taxable brokerage withdrawals trigger capital gains tax on the realized portion; pretax IRA/401(k) withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income; Roth and HSA (qualified) withdraw tax-free.

401(k) vs Traditional IRA: In this calculator these two types are treated almost identically — both grow tax-deferred, both are taxed as ordinary income on withdrawal, and both trigger RMDs at age 73. The one difference is that 401(k) contributions entered in General Settings are deducted from taxable salary each year while working. Traditional IRA contributions are not currently deducted from salary — if you contribute to a Traditional IRA, either use the 401(k) type and enter your contribution in General Settings, or manually reduce your salary by the contribution amount. Contribution limits, employer match, and still-working RMD exceptions are not modeled.

Withdrawals & Taxes

Configure how the model funds your spending each year. The order below decides which account is drained first to cover expenses (and to cover the tax bill on those withdrawals). Federal taxes are computed using the bracket tables; Social Security taxability uses the simplified MFJ rules; both standard deduction and brackets inflate each year using your default inflation rate.

Withdrawal Strategy

Drag with ↑ ↓ to reorder.

Typical recommended order: Taxable brokerage first (capital gains rates, no deferred tax), then Inherited IRA (mandatory 10-year drain — placing it early depletes it faster than required), then Traditional IRA / 401(k) (ordinary income tax on withdrawal), then Roth last (tax-free growth, no RMDs — preserve as long as possible).
⚠️ Placing Inherited IRA first causes spending withdrawals to drain it well ahead of the IRS 10-year schedule.

    Inherited IRA Strategy

    Under the SECURE Act (post-2019, non-spouse beneficiaries), inherited IRAs must be fully drained within 10 years of the original owner's death. Every dollar distributed counts as ordinary income in the year taken — so the goal is to spread distributions across low-tax years rather than bunching them late and hitting a high bracket all at once.

    Each year the model first takes any required minimum distribution (RMD) — a proportional amount that keeps the account on track to empty by year 10. Then, during the Roth conversion bracket-fill window, any remaining bracket headroom (ordinary income capacity before hitting your target bracket ceiling) can be used to drain more of the inherited IRA, do Roth conversions, or both. The strategy setting below controls how that headroom is split.

    Sequence of Returns Risk

    Sequence of returns risk is the danger that a market downturn early in retirement — when your portfolio is at its peak and withdrawals begin — permanently impairs your plan even if long-run average returns are fine. A bad sequence in the first 5–10 years of retirement forces you to sell assets at depressed prices to cover living expenses, leaving fewer shares to recover when markets rebound. The retirement-year liquid asset percentiles above show how much variability exists at the most vulnerable moment: the P10 ("worst 10%") scenario represents a severely impaired starting position that is very difficult to recover from, even if subsequent returns are average.

    Financial Independence, Retire Early (FIRE)

    FIRE is a movement built around saving and investing aggressively — typically 50–70% of income — to reach financial independence decades before traditional retirement age. The core idea: accumulate enough assets so investment returns cover your living expenses indefinitely, freeing you from needing a paycheck.

    🪨 Lean FIRE
    Bare-bones financial freedom
    Achieve financial independence with a very lean budget — typically under $40,000/year. Requires strict frugality and minimal discretionary spending. Popular with those who can genuinely thrive with a simple lifestyle.
    Target & SWR25× your annual lean expenses (4% SWR). Generally $500k–$1M for very low-cost lifestyles.
    🔥 Classic FIRE
    The original formula
    The foundational FIRE number: accumulate 25× your expected annual expenses. Based on the Trinity Study's finding that a 4% annual withdrawal rate is historically sustainable over 30+ year retirement horizons.
    Target & SWR25× annual expenses at a 4% SWR. The benchmark most FIRE calculators use.
    🌊 Coast FIRE
    Save hard now, coast to retirement
    Reach a portfolio large enough that — if you stop contributing and let it grow — it will reach your full FIRE number by traditional retirement age. After hitting Coast FIRE, you only need to earn enough to cover current living expenses (no more saving required).
    Target & SWRFull FIRE number discounted back at your expected return rate to today. The earlier you want to coast, the smaller the number.
    ☕ Barista FIRE
    Semi-retire with part-time income
    Retire from your full-time career but work part-time to cover a portion of expenses. The part-time income reduces the portfolio you need. Named for the idea of working a low-stress job (like a barista) that also provides benefits like health insurance.
    Target & SWRPortfolio covers (annual expenses − part-time income) × 25. Smaller target than Classic FIRE in exchange for continued part-time work.
    🥩 Fat FIRE
    Retire with abundance
    Financial independence with a high income lifestyle — typically $100,000+/year in retirement spending. Requires a larger nest egg but affords travel, dining, hobbies, and a comfortable life without budget anxiety.
    Target & SWR25× a generous annual budget (typically $100k–$200k+/yr). Usually $2.5M–$5M+.
